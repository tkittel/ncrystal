1. ncrystalelastic.pdf
=====================================================================
Citation: T. Kittelmann, X.-X. Cai, "Elastic neutron scattering models for NCrystal", Computer Physics Communications 267 (2021) 108082, DOI 10.1016/j.cpc.2021.108082 (CC-BY; received Dec 2020, published Jul 2021). 30 pages.

Scope: Documents the elastic models in NCrystal v1.0.0-v2.7.0 (figures made with v2.0.0): Bragg diffraction in powders, in single crystals with isotropic Gaussian mosaicity, in layered single crystals (pyrolytic graphite), and incoherent elastic scattering. Focus on implementation details, efficiency and validation (mpmath high-precision references, consistency checks, comparison to Sears/Wuttke/Frikkee). Byproduct: improved closed-form approximation (Eq. 35) for single-crystal Bragg cross sections beyond the usual Gaussian approximation. No extinction, no texture, no filter tables: those are explicitly listed as *future* work.

NCrystal features/cfg params explicitly mentioned: `mosprec` (eps, default 1e-3), `dcutoff` (default 0.1 Aa; 0.25 Aa for Y2O3), `sccutoff` (default 0.4 Aa), `lcmode` (0 default; n>0 evenly spaced rotations; n<0 random), temperature default 293.15 K; v2.1.0 user-modifiable atom data; v2.2.0 plugins; v2.6.0 atomic displacements from VDOS instead of Debye temperature; multi-phase cfg-strings and multithread safety announced as future work.

Models / formulas:
- General Bragg (Sec. 2): per-plane d2sigma/dOmega dE = (2pi)^3/(V_uc n_a) delta(dE) delta(Q - tau_hkl) |F_hkl|^2. Bragg condition lambda <= 2d; theta_B; alpha = pi/2 - theta_B = angle between -k_i and plane normal; "Bragg circle" of radius sin(alpha). Averaging over mosaicity W(n_hat): sigma_hkl = q_hkl * g_hkl, with q_hkl = lambda^3 |F|^2 / (V_uc n_a sin 2theta_B) and g_hkl = sin(alpha) * Int_{-pi}^{pi} W(mu=cos alpha, t) dt. Sampling: pick plane by relative xs, sample normal via P(t) ~ W(cos alpha, t), specular reflection k_f = k_i + tau n_hat.
- Powder (Sec. 3): W = 1/4pi -> g = sin(alpha)/2 -> sigma_hkl = lambda^2 d |F|^2 / (2 V_uc n_a); sigma_powder(lambda) = lambda^2/(2 V_uc n_a) * sum_{lambda<=2d} d_hkl |F_hkl|^2 (Eq. 13). O(log N_hkl) via binary search in arrays of E_edge = h^2/(8 m d^2) and cumulative sums of d|F|^2 (merged by equal d); scattering sampled by second binary search on cumulative sums; cos(theta) = 1 - 2 h^2/(8 m d^2 E); azimuth uniform; k_f built without trig calls via isotropic unit vector + cross product (Eq. 15). ~50 MHz xs, ~10 MHz sampling. Validated in the 2020 CPC framework paper.
- Isotropic Gaussian mosaic single crystal (Sec. 4): W(delta) = N exp(-delta^2/2sigma^2) Theta(tau - delta), truncation tau = max(3, 1.1 sqrt(-2 ln eps)) sigma (eps=1e-3 -> tau=4.0886 sigma; tau=5 sigma at eps=3.262e-5), required tau < pi/2; N from N^-1 = 2pi Int_0^tau exp(-theta^2/2sigma^2) sin theta dtheta (numerically to 1e-12). Geometric integral g = 2 N sin(alpha) Int_0^{t'} exp(-[arccos(sin a sin g cos t + cos a cos g)]^2/2sigma^2) dt (Eq. 24), gamma = angle(n_hat, -k_i), delta_0 = |alpha - gamma|, t' = arccos(max(-1, (cos tau - cos a cos g)/(sin a sin g))). Pre-selection (Sec. 4.3): planes grouped into families (same d, |F|); hkl/-h-k-l pairs handled jointly; contribution test (1-cos^2 a)(1-cos^2 g) > [max(0, cos tau - cos a cos g)]^2 (Eq. 26/27, branch-free). Evaluation (Sec. 4.4): cubic-spline lookup of f(x)=N exp(-arccos(x)^2/2sigma^2) on [cos tau, 1]; custom Romberg starting with 17 equidistant points, cos t by addition formulas. Closed-form approximation (Eq. 35): g ~= [(2pi sigma^2)^-1/2 exp(-delta_0^2/2sigma^2)] * erf(sqrt((tau^2-delta_0^2)/2sigma^2)) * sqrt(sin alpha / sin gamma) * N/(1/(2pi sigma^2)); i.e. simple Gaussian times truncation erf, curvature factor sqrt(sin a/sin g) (asymmetric rocking curves, matches Wuttke's 1 - eta cot(alpha)/2 to lowest order) and normalisation correction. Implemented as g = S(cos(alpha-gamma)) sqrt(sin a/sin g) with spline S (Eq. 36). Used only when tau < psi and cos psi sin g sin a + cos a cos g < cos tau (psi ~ 22.6 deg at eps=1e-3), else Romberg fallback. Sampling (4.5): rejection sampling of t in [-t', t'] with constant overlay (max at t=0), acceptance 15-35 %. Short d-spacing (4.6): planes with d < sccutoff (0.4 Aa) treated with the powder model (isotropic approx), 1-2 orders of magnitude speedup.
- Layered single crystal / pyrolytic graphite (Sec. 5): g_LC(alpha, k_i) = (1/2pi) Int_{-pi}^{pi} g_SC(alpha, gamma(phi)) dphi with cos gamma = sin theta_k sin theta_n cos phi + cos theta_n cos theta_k (theta_k = angle(-k_i, L), theta_n = angle(n, L)); planes grouped by (d, theta_n); theta_n and pi-theta_n groups processed jointly; z-range precheck [cos(theta_k+alpha), cos(max(0,theta_k-alpha))]; non-zero phi interval phi_min/phi_max from Eq. (49/50): cos phi between (cos(alpha+tau) - cos tk cos tn)/(sin tk sin tn) and (cos(max(0,alpha-tau)) - cos tk cos tn)/(sin tk sin tn); Romberg over [phi_min, phi_max] with Eq. 36 inside. Sampling of phi: 9-point piecewise-constant overlay (x1.7 and +2 % safety), acceptance 30-40 %, then sign flip 50 %. Reference model (5.4, `lcmode`): average of n rotated non-layered crystals via Rodrigues rotations; needs 2pi/n ~ 5 % of FWHM so n ~ 7e3 (1 deg) to 2.6e6 (10"); McStas Single_crystal PG mode corresponds to lcmode=-1 and is shown inadequate. Cross-section structure analysed (sigma ~ lambda^2/sqrt(1-(lambda/2d)^2) plateaus, triangular regions, Fig 19-22).
- Incoherent elastic (Sec. 6): dsigma/dOmega = sum_j sigma_inc_j/(4pi) exp(-Q^2 delta_j^2) (isotropic harmonic DW, W=Q^2 delta^2/2); sigma_inc,el = sigma_inc (1 - e^-t)/t with t = (2 k delta)^2 = (4 pi delta/lambda)^2 (Eq. 56/57); 3rd-order Taylor for t<0.01, sigma_inc/t for t>24; mu=cos theta sampled from P(mu) = N_t exp(t mu/2), N_t = t/(4 sinh(t/2)); rejection sampling for t<0.02, else mu = -1 + (2/t) ln(1 + (e^t - 1) R). Shows isotropic approx fails at 1.8 Aa for most materials (Fig 35), validated vs EXFOR V and Ni total xs.
- Debye-Waller: isotropic harmonic, MSD from Debye temperature (Appendix A tables of Debye T and delta at 293.15 K) in the paper's version; v2.6.0 switched to VDOS-derived.
- Efficiency (Sec. 7): baseline 500 MHz, inc-el/powder xs ~50 MHz, sampling ~10 MHz, single crystal 1-10 MHz above 1.5 Aa, 0.1-1 MHz below; layered ~10x slower than single crystal.

Validation: mpmath 100-digit references for Eq. 24 rocking curves (sigma = 1 deg, 1', back-scattering alpha = sigma); azimuthal sampling distributions (Figs 9-11 incl. back/forward scattering with -n contribution); "zig-zag" walk 1e10 steps; isotropic illumination of a single crystal reproduces powder xs to <1e-3 (1e-4 discrepancy at 10 deg mosaicity, vanishes at eps=1e-5); reflectivity of Ge-511 slabs vs Sears (Darwin-Hamilton, in-plane) and Wuttke (out-of-plane MC): agreement; Sears fails at large mosaicity/high Bragg angle, Wuttke unstable at 0.01 deg. PG: vs lcmode reference (eps 1e-7, 1e7 rotations), Frikkee's analytical peak positions, and Frikkee's measured transmission through 16 mm PG slab at 2.929 Aa (remarkable agreement; needs inelastic + inc-el + absorption too).

Limitations stated: ideally imperfect crystal (Born approx., no extinction); harmonic isotropic DW; Gaussian mosaicity only isotropic (no anisotropic mosaic or d-spacing fluctuation like McStas Single_crystal); tau<pi/2 (sigma <= 14.4 deg at eps=1e-7); spline artefacts near truncation edges; no finite grain size, lattice-parameter fluctuations, site occupancy, chemical disorder, strain, SANS, or texture in polycrystals (all listed as possible future features); multi-phase only via user code.

2. Kittelmann2026 ... Revisiting Becker-Coppens (1974)
=====================================================================
Citation: T. Kittelmann, D. D. DiJulio, S. Xu, J. I. Marquez Damian, "Revisiting Becker-Coppens (1974): updated recipes for estimating extinction factors in spherical crystallites", Acta Cryst. (2026) A82, DOI 10.1107/S2053273326001245 (received 20 Nov 2025, accepted 5 Feb 2026; 16 pages + supporting info; code/data at Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.18493059 with C, C++ and Python reference implementations).

Scope: Purely numerical correction of the BC1974 recipes for the extinction factor y = I_obs/I_kin in perfect spherical crystallites/grains. No change of physics: same integral, same four models (primary P; secondary with Gaussian G, Lorentzian L, Fresnellian F mosaic). Shows BC1974 recipes break down (y>1, non-monotonic in crystallite radius) at strong extinction and high Bragg angle; provides "standard" (relerr < 1e-3) and "luxury" (< 1e-6) recipes valid for all theta and x <= 1000 with power-law extrapolation beyond. New recipes = BC2025.

Key formulas:
- y_M(x,theta) = (6 c_M / 4) Int_0^inf f_M(eta) phi(c_M x f_M(eta), theta) deta (Eq. 2); c_P=c_G=c_F=1, c_L=4/3. Extinction correction function phi(s,theta) = phi_0(s) + sin^(3/2)theta [phi_pi(s sqrt(sin theta)) - phi_0(s sqrt(sin theta))]; phi_0(s) = 3/(64 s^3) [8 s^2 + 4 s exp(-4s) - (1 - exp(-4s))]; phi_pi(s) = 3/(4 s^3) [s^2 - s + (1/2) ln(1+2s)] (note: BC1974 Eq. 32 has a misplaced factor 1/2 - corrected here).
- Profiles: f_P(eta) = [eta^2 - eta sin(2eta) + sin^2(eta)]/eta^4; f_G = exp(-9 eta^2/(16 pi)); f_L = 1/(1+eta^2); f_F = sin^2(3eta/4)/(3eta/4)^2.
- Exact separation (Eq. 27): y_M(x,theta) = y0_M(x) + sin^(3/2)theta * yDelta_M(x sqrt(sin theta)), with y0 = y(x,0), yDelta = y(x,pi/2) - y(x,0). So only two 1-D functions per model need parameterising; recipes take sin(theta) as argument (cheap from Bragg condition).
- Taylor series near x=0 (Eqs. 8-11), e.g. y_P ~ 1 - (33/35) x + ..., y_G ~ 1 - (3 sqrt2/4) x, y_L ~ 1 - x, y_F ~ 1 - x. Hence correct small-x constants t_P = 66/35 ~ 1.89 (BC1974 used 2 -> 6.1 % error), t_G = 3/sqrt2, t_L = t_F = 2.
- BC1974 recipe form (Eq. 17): y = [1 + t_M x + A_M(theta) x^2 / (1 + B_M(theta) x)]^(-1/2) with A_P = 0.20 + 0.45 cos 2theta, B_P = 0.22 - 0.12 (0.5 - cos 2theta)^2, A_G = 0.58 + 0.48 cos2theta + 0.24 cos^2 2theta, B_G = 0.02 - 0.025 cos 2theta, A_L = 0.025 + 0.285 cos 2theta, B_L = 0.15 - 0.2 (0.75 - cos 2theta)^2 (cos2theta>=0) / -0.45 cos 2theta (else; discontinuous), A_F = 0.48 + 0.6 cos 2theta, B_F = 0.20 - 0.6 (0.2 - cos 2theta)^2. Square-root argument goes negative at high x -> breakdown; tuned only on 0.1<=x<=30, sin theta 0.05-0.9 (0.8 for P); reference tables of BC1974 themselves inaccurate (single Gauss quadrature).
- New recipe structure (Algorithm 1 for y_P standard, Appendix D Algorithms 2-8): x<0.1 (luxury: 0.02): 5th-order Taylor; x>1000: y(1000) * (1000/x)^p with p=0.5 (P,L,F) or 0.933 (G); else x' = (sqrt x - 1)/(sqrt x + 1), polynomial (Horner, from Legendre fits) in x' for y0 (with transform y' = [y + x'(3-x'^2)/4 - 1/2]/[(1+x')^2 (1-x')]) and for yDelta in u = x sqrt(sin theta); return y0 + sin theta * sqrt(sin theta) * yDelta. Double precision required.
- Reference evaluation: mpmath 150 digits, Gauss-Legendre over many sub-intervals, Taylor expansions (30/60/60/20 orders) for small x; tails: G negligible beyond eta=1000; L asymptotic series in 1/a (Eq. 12-15, a = 2+4x, convergent for a > 1 + 8x/3); P bounded by f+- = (eta^2 +- eta + 1)/eta^4 giving tail ~ 1/a - x/(2a^3) +- (1/(2a^2) + 2/a^3), needing a = 2.4e4-1.2e5; F via Richardson extrapolation over intervals [(4pi/3) n, (4pi/3)(n+1)] (brute-force check: 300 CPU-days per point, n=1e9).
- Precision metric: relerr = |y - y_ref| / min(y_ref, 1 - y_ref).

NCrystal statements (flag): Fig. 1 shows "Bragg diffraction neutron cross sections in a beryllium powder as calculated by NCrystal, including primary extinction effects for idealized spherical crystallites" (r=0,2,5,10 um) with both BC1974 and BC2025 -> extinction-capable NCrystal (or plugin) exists in the authors' hands; "since NCrystal caps y values at 1 this shows up as a sharp kink". Introduction: BC model "was naturally considered by the authors when working on implementing extinction models in NCrystal ... to facilitate the modelling and analysis of Bragg edge transmission and diffraction data." Conclusions: "In the near future, these updated recipes will be adopted into NCrystal". No cfg parameters or version numbers given. The mapping of x to physical parameters (r, g, etc.) is deferred to BC1974 / Xu 2025.

Limitations: no new physics; x>1000 not precision-guaranteed; BC model assumptions (spherical crystallites, Darwin transfer equations) unchanged; modelling of extinction "by no means a solved subject".

3. Xu 2025 JAC, extinction in solid beryllium
=====================================================================
Citation: S. Xu, D. D. DiJulio, J. I. Marquez Damian, S. C. Vogel, A. M. Long, T. Y. Hirsh, T. Kittelmann, V. Kuksenko, G. Muhrer, "Impact of extinction effects on neutron transmission in solid beryllium metal", J. Appl. Cryst. (2025) 58, 1957-1966, DOI 10.1107/S1600576725007939. (Duplicate file: shuqi_2025_....pdf.)

Scope: TOF transmission (LumaCam event camera) + simultaneous HIPPO diffraction (MAUD/E-WIMV texture) on four Be grades (S-200-F, S-65, S-65-H, BHPR; 1 cm cubes, 30 min each, two orientations). Measured total xs below ideal-polycrystal prediction, as in all EXFOR Be data; explained by a lattice-plane-dependent Becker-Coppens extinction correction (primary + secondary), with texture ruled out (except BHPR which has strong (002) fibre texture).

Model (Sec. 3, "RITS" method of Sato et al. 2011):
- sigma_coh,el(lambda) = lambda^2/(2V) sum_{lambda<=2d} d_hkl F_hkl^2 E_hkl(lambda, F_hkl) (Eq. 2) - i.e. NCrystal's powder formula times per-plane extinction factor.
- E_hkl = E_p(x_p) * E_s[E_p(x_p) x_s] (Eq. 3): secondary acts on already primary-extinguished beam.
- E_p(x_p) = [1 + 2 x_p + A_p(theta) x_p^2/(1 + B_p(theta) x_p)]^(-1/2) (Eq. 4), E_s[y] = [1 + 2.12 y x_s + A_s(theta) (y x_s)^2/(1 + B_s(theta) y x_s)]^(-1/2) with y = E_p (Eq. 5); A, B from BC1974 (Gaussian secondary). These are the BC1974 recipes criticised in paper 2.
- x_p = (2/3) Q_lambda t^2 sin(2theta)/lambda (Eq. 6); x_s = (2/3) Q_lambda T { [t sin(2theta)/lambda]^2 + 1/(2 g^2) }^(-1/2) (Eq. 7); Q_lambda = lambda^3 F_hkl^2 /(V^2 sin 2theta) (Eq. 8); t = (3/2) r (crystallite), T = (3/2) R (grain) average path lengths for spheres; g = 1/(2 sqrt(pi) eta), eta = std dev of Gaussian mosaic of crystallites within a grain. Limits: type-I x_s,I = (2 sqrt2/3) g Q_lambda T (mosaic-dominated, crystallite size irrelevant), type-II x_s,II = (2/3) Q_lambda t T sin(2theta)/lambda (size-dominated). Pure secondary: E_p = 1.
- Flag: "The above formalism has been implemented in a plugin named CrysExtn (Xu, 2024), integrated into the NCrystal toolkit" (https://github.com/XuShuqi7/ncplugin-CrysExtn). NCrystal also used for normalisation at 0.5 Aa (free-atom xs) and for the Be Debye-Waller factor B = 0.4311 Aa^2 from VDOS (within 2 % of Peng et al. 1996). Conclusions: "we employed Becker and Coppens' extinction model using the toolkit NCrystal combined with the CrysExtn plugin". No core cfg parameter names given.

Fitting/validation: lmfit least squares of sigma_tot(lambda) = -c_t ln(I/I0), with Jorgensen/Von Dreele asymmetric edge profile for instrument resolution (7 params, several fixed to 0); 2R fixed from EBSD grain size (6.5-10.1 um); free: 2r and g. Results: fitted 2r always < 2R (max r/R = 1/3 for S-65-H); reduced chi2 ~2 (S-200-F, S-65, S-65-H), ~8 for textured BHPR. S-200-F model comparison: primary+secondary chi2 3.4? (listed: both 3.4, pure secondary 1.7?, type-I 4.0, type-II 3.1 - text: "3.4, 1.7, 4.0 and 3.1" for pure primary, pure secondary, type-I, type-II respectively); pure-primary-only gives 2r = 3.16 um, others ~1 um; pure secondary fits as well as combined with 15 % larger crystallite. Table 1: at lambda = 2d, E_p 0.95-0.996 and E_s 0.70-0.97 for S-200-F; S-65-H E_p down to 0.845 (002). t/extinction-length ratio (Lambda = V/(F lambda)) 0.07-0.56. Type-I better < 2.5 Aa, type-II better at long wavelengths. Mosaic (g) rounds the largest Bragg edge near 3.5 Aa; crystallite size (r) keeps sharp edges. Good agreement with EXFOR data (Gerasimov hot-pressed vs extruded, Harvey, Liu, Fermi 1947).

Limitations: model-dependent effective parameters (2r, g, 2R); BC model questionable when r approaches R (Cooper & Rouse 1976 alternative fits 2.4x worse); Darwin equations first-order approx (Kulda); random grain orientation assumed (fails for textured BHPR); uses the flawed BC1974 recipes (motivating paper 2).

4. Dollase 1986 (March-Dollase)
=====================================================================
Citation: W. A. Dollase, "Correction of intensities for preferred orientation in powder diffractometry: application of the March model", J. Appl. Cryst. (1986) 19, 267-272 (DOI 10.1107/S0021889886089458, not printed in the PDF). 6 pages.

Scope: For axially symmetric (flat-plate or capillary) samples of effectively disk- or rod-shaped crystallites, preferred orientation is corrected with a single 1-D pole-density profile P_H(alpha) of the preferred-orientation plane (HKL); proposes the March (1932) function as the profile.

Formulas:
- I_h = s L P_h(0) F_h^2; P_h(0) = P_H(alpha), alpha = angle between (hkl) and (HKL) (flat plate). Capillary: P_H(alpha) = (2/pi) Int_0^{pi/2} P_H(alpha, beta) dbeta with phi = arccos(sin alpha sin beta).
- March-Dollase: P(alpha) = (r^2 cos^2 alpha + r^-1 sin^2 alpha)^(-3/2) (Table 1 no. 6). Single parameter r; r<1 max at alpha=0 (platy, flat plate), r>1 max at pi/2; range r^-3 to r^(3/2)... (text: "pole density ranges from r^-3 to r^(3/2)"); normalised to unit integral over the sphere, smooth at 0 and pi/2; r = d/d0 (platy) or d0/d (acicular) = compaction ratio.
- Symmetry-equivalent planes: I = s L [sum_i P_H(alpha_i)] F^2 (generalised multiplicity). Two-population extension: I = s L [x P_H1(alpha_1) + (1-x) P_H2(alpha_2)] F^2.
- Alternatives compared (Table 1): Gaussian exp(-G alpha^2) (Rietveld), exp(G(pi/2-alpha)^2), exp(-G sin^2 alpha), exp(-G(1-cos^a alpha)), Gaussian+constant; Gaussian underestimates high-angle pole density.
Validation: calcite (r=0.89) and huntite (r=0.79 -> 21 % compaction; slurried r=0.57 -> 43 %, 6x max density) refinements; March gives R 5.3 % vs 7.6 % Gaussian. Limitations: axial symmetry, single preferred plane, rod/disk crystallites; capillary transform differs for strong texture. Relevant to NCrystal as the standard one-parameter texture model (not implemented in NCrystal per the 2021 paper's outlook).

5. Sabine 1985 and Sabine 1988
=====================================================================
(a) T. M. Sabine, "Extinction in polycrystalline materials", Aust. J. Phys. (1985) 38, 507-518 (scanned, no DOI printed; DOI 10.1071/PH850507).
Scope: primary extinction factor y for powders of spherical perfect grains, from the Darwin/Hamilton energy-transfer equations, extrapolating to dynamical theory for the symmetric Bragg plate; tested with neutron diffraction on sintered MgO of controlled grain size (0.7-20 um, 1.088 Aa), agreement to y = 0.60. States secondary extinction has no meaning in a random powder (replaced by multiple scattering); only primary extinction treated.
Formulas: Laue plate I_f = I0/2 [1 - exp(-2 sigma D)], D = D0/cos theta; Bragg plate I_f = I0 sigma D/(1 + sigma D), D = D0/sin theta; coupling sigma(dk) = Q_k times profile: Lorentzian Q_k T/[1 + (pi T dk)^2], Fresnellian Q_k T sin^2(pi T dk)/(pi T dk)^2, Gaussian Q_k T exp(-pi (T dk)^2), rectangular, triangular (T = volume-averaged thickness normal to the plane; integral breadth gives Scherrer). With x = Q_k D T: Laue y_L = 1 - x/2 + x^2/4 - 5x^3/48 ..., y_F = 1 - 2x/3 + 11x^2/30 - 151x^3/945, y_G = 1 - 2x/(2!2^1/2) + 4x^2/(3!3^1/2) - ..., y_R = (1/2x)(1 - e^-2x), y_T = (1/x)[1 - (1/2x)(1 - e^-2x)]; Bragg y_L = (1+x)^(-1/2), y_F = 1 - 2x/3 + 11x^2/20 - 151x^3/315, y_G = 1 - x/2^1/2 + x^2/3^1/2 - x^3/4^1/2, y_R = 1/(1+x), y_T = (2/x^2)(x - ln|1+x|). Dynamical Bragg plate y = tanh(A)/A, A = N_c lambda F D0/sin theta = x^1/2; Lorentzian Bragg extrapolates correctly to x^-1/2 (thick-crystal limit), so Lorentzian chosen. Sphere (Bragg case, Wilkins' T = l0 sin theta rule): T = (3/2) r, D = (3/2) r sin theta -> x = (9/4) N_c^2 lambda^2 F^2 r^2 (Eq. 10), y = (1+x)^(-1/2); small-x: y = 1 - (9/8) F^2 N_c^2 lambda^2 r^2 (cf. Ekstein 7/4, Becker-Coppens 99/70). Rietveld-ready form (conclusions): y = (1+x)^(-1/2), x = ((3/4) N_c F lambda D)^2 with D grain diameter; polydisperse: y = (sum f_i D_i^3)^-1 sum f_i D_i^3 (1 + x_i)^(-1/2). Note x is theta-independent here (Bragg-case assumption for all angles). Validation: MgO 200/400/600 reflections, y_exp 0.55-0.73 vs y_th 0.59-0.72 (Table 2); apparent B factor decreases with grain size. Limitations: Bragg-case only, no absorption, no secondary extinction, multiple scattering (~10 %) assumed equal across specimens, grain = perfect crystal assumed.

(b) T. M. Sabine, "A reconciliation of extinction theories", Acta Cryst. (1988) A44, 368-374, DOI 10.1107/S0108767388000613 (file also contains first page of Sabine, Von Dreele & Jorgensen 1988, A44, 374-379: TOF MgO test to y = 0.3 with GSAS).
Scope: shows Hamilton, Zachariasen, Werner, Becker-Coppens and Sabine all solve the same Darwin-Hamilton transfer equations (dP_i/dt_i = tau P_i + sigma P_f, dP_f/dt_f = tau P_f + sigma P_i) and differ only in the chosen coupling profile sigma; derives a size-limit constraint and an angular interpolation.
Key points/formulas: asymptotic requirement y -> proportional to (entrance area growth)/(volume growth): y ~ 1/(l0 sin 2theta) for the AA parallelepiped, 1/a for mosaic block, 1/r for sphere and cylinder. Therefore for primary extinction (x = Q_k T D) only Lorentzian or Fresnellian profiles are admissible; for secondary extinction (x = Q_theta G D, G = W(0) = 1/(eta sqrt(2pi)) for Gaussian mosaic of std eta) only Gaussian, rectangular or triangular. Bragg/Laue y lists as in 1985 (Eqs. 6-15) plus Laue Lorentzian asymptotic y = (2/(pi x))^1/2 [1 - 1/(8x) - 3/(128 x^2) - ...]; limits y_B -> x^-1/2 = A^-1, y_L -> (2/(pi x))^1/2 (vs dynamical (2A)^-1); x = A^2 in both cases. AA crystal: T = l0 sin theta, D = l0, x = (N_c lambda F l0)^2. Sphere: T = D = (3/2) r? ("T = D = 3r/2" giving x = 9 (N_c lambda F r)^2/(4 sin theta)) vs Zachariasen x = 3 (N_c lambda F r)^2/(2 sin 2theta). Angular dependence (Eq. 17): y(2theta) = y_L cos^2 theta + y_B sin^2 theta. Hamilton (rectangular, cylinder, x scaled by 3pi/8) and BC (x_BC = x/2, extrapolates (2x)^-1/2) reproduced by this interpolation (Fig. 3). BC include sin 2theta in secondary x, Hamilton/Werner/Sabine do not - left open. Validation: MgO 1985 (y to 0.6) and TOF 1988 (y to 0.3). This is the basis of the Sabine extinction model in GSAS/International Tables Vol. C (Sabine 2006), the alternative that Palmer & Jauch 1995 found inferior to BC (per Xu 2025).

- Extinction enters the powder model multiplicatively per plane: sigma_hkl -> sigma_hkl * y_hkl(x_hkl, theta_B), with x ~ |F|^2 lambda^2 (or lambda^3/sin2theta) * size^2 / V^2 (primary) or with mosaic g and grain size (secondary); Xu 2025 gives the explicit BC parameterisation (Eqs. 6-8), Sabine 1985 gives the simpler y = (1+x)^-1/2 Lorentzian/Bragg-case form.
- Paper 2 supplies drop-in replacements for the BC1974 E_p/E_s recipes used in Xu 2025 / CrysExtn; the paper says adoption into NCrystal is planned, and its Fig. 1 already shows NCrystal-computed Be powder xs with primary extinction (r up to 10 um) and y capped at 1.
- Texture: none of the NCrystal papers implement it; Dollase gives the March-Dollase P(alpha) = (r^2 cos^2 alpha + sin^2 alpha / r)^-3/2 which would replace the constant W = 1/4pi in Eq. 10 of the 2021 paper for an axially symmetric fibre texture (Xu 2025 shows BHPR Be needs it).
